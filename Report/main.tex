\begin{abstract}
Weak gravitational lensing uses galaxy-shape correlations to study dark
matter and cosmic expansion, but intrinsic alignments also contribute to
these correlations. Allowing uncertain alignment responses to vary across
scale and redshift can introduce many adjustable parameters. Can a compact
representation preserve this flexibility without losing important features?

We construct a controlled family of positive alignment-response surfaces
inspired by the nonlinear alignment (NLA) model, with 13 parameters governing redshift evolution and scale
dependence. Numerical checks of random combinations and all parameter-domain
corners precede the generation of 100,000 surfaces on a $31\times101$ grid.
Using common training and validation samples, we compare principal component
analysis (PCA), a convolutional autoencoder (Conv1D), and a PCA-assisted
autoencoder at two to ten latent coordinates. Our investigation combines
interpretable model design, matched-dimension reconstruction tests, and
diagnostics of where compression fails.

The reported three-layer Conv1D model with six coordinates recovers
$99.92\%$ of the validation log-amplitude variance and has a mean relative
amplitude error of $1.21\%$, a $41\%$ reduction from six-component PCA.
Ten Conv1D coordinates reduce the mean error to $0.23\%$. PCA-assisted
compression achieves comparable averages with substantially fewer trainable
parameters. However, the six-coordinate Conv1D model still reaches a
$26.36\%$ local error, showing that high variance recovery does not ensure
uniform accuracy. Latent correlations reveal associations with model
parameters but do not establish a unique physical interpretation.

These results demonstrate useful nonlinear compression within the sampled
response family and identify the residual errors that further development
must address. They are single-seed validation results; final test evaluation
and propagation into lensing observables remain necessary. The method offers
a concrete starting point for compact representations of alignment
uncertainty in future weak-lensing analyses.
\end{abstract}
