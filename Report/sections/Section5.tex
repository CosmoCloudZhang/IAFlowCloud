\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

We first compare reconstruction accuracy at equal latent dimension, then
examine saved notebook diagnostics of an earlier run of the compact
Conv1D architecture, identified in Section~\ref{sec:results-residuals}.
Comparison scores use the same 10,000 validation surfaces. The comparison
contains one training trajectory per neural configuration, with seed 42;
it is not an independent final-test assessment.

\subsection{Model comparison and a compact working choice}
\label{sec:results-comparison}

The comparison includes PCA and three capacity tiers of both Conv1D and
PCA-AE at $L\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$. The neural models use the common training
sample and optimization settings of Section~\ref{sec:training-selection},
with the stated differences in model size and epoch budget. The three-layer
Conv1D results use the September run series
\texttt{20260909-003908} at every width, replacing the earlier same-seed
series consistently. The reported scores are taken from the saved
validation artifacts in the project repository, with the principal
comparisons listed in Table~\ref{tab:validation-results}.
The separate Conv2D experiment has an incomplete grid and is outside this
comparison; no claim about its relative performance is made here.

Figure~\ref{fig:overall-validation} shows that both nonlinear methods have
lower log RMSE and mean relative amplitude error than rank-$L$ PCA at every
reported width. For the three-layer Conv1D model, the mean relative error
falls from $5.30\%$ at $L=2$ to $1.21\%$ at $L=6$ and $0.23\%$ at
$L=10$. At $L=6$, the reduction relative to PCA is $41.2\%$ in mean
relative error and $42.1\%$ in log RMSE. Nonlinear compression therefore
preserves this response family more accurately at the same representation
length. This comparison concerns approximation quality; it does not imply
that neural encoding is faster or uses less shared model storage than PCA.

Increasing depth does not give a consistent improvement. For example, at
$L=6$ the five-layer Conv1D model reduces mean relative error to $1.15\%$,
compared with $1.21\%$ for three layers, but its global maximum error
increases from $26.36\%$ to $43.67\%$. At $L=10$, the three-layer model
has the lowest log RMSE of the reported configurations. Depth here also
changes the dense layers, so the comparison tests capacity tiers rather
than isolating convolutional depth. Multiple independent seeds would be
needed to distinguish systematic architectural effects from training
variability.

The tail metrics in Figure~\ref{fig:tail-validation} also improve over
matched-rank PCA, but the ordering among neural configurations depends on
the metric and width. At $L=6$, the three-layer Conv1D and PCA-AE models
have almost identical 95th-percentile surface relative RMS errors
($3.14\%$ for both after rounding) and 99th-percentile surface maximum
errors ($16.64\%$ and $16.79\%$). Their single largest errors differ more:
$26.36\%$ for Conv1D and $48.07\%$ for PCA-AE. A lower percentile does
not guarantee a lower absolute maximum; for example, the five-layer
Conv1D model at $L=2$ has a worse global maximum than rank-2 PCA despite
better average and percentile errors.

  \label{fig:tail-validation}
\end{figure}

For detailed diagnostics we retain the three-layer Conv1D model at $L=6$
as a compact working architecture: six is the first tested width to meet the
average target, three layers give the smallest Conv1D model at that width,
and this run has a smaller observed global maximum than its three-layer
PCA-AE counterpart. It recovers $99.92\%$ of the validation variance with
log RMSE $0.00776$ dex. Ten-component PCA is somewhat more accurate on
average ($99.94\%$ recovered variance and $1.04\%$ mean relative error),
so six learned coordinates approach, but do not exceed, that linear
baseline. The choice is provisional and does not freeze the final-test
configuration.

PCA-AE remains a useful alternative. At $L=6$, its three-layer model has
51,076 trainable parameters, about forty times fewer than Conv1D's
2,041,989, with nearly the same average accuracy. It also stores a fixed
rank-30 basis. Thus the preferred method depends on representation length,
shared model size, and sensitivity to rare large errors. No measured
runtime advantage is claimed. Table~\ref{tab:validation-results}
summarizes the principal numerical comparisons.

\subsection{PCA and the distribution of surface variation}
\label{sec:results-pca}

PCA helps explain why compression is possible. Figure~\ref{fig:pca-variance}
shows how strongly the training surfaces vary along each principal
component. The first component accounts for $86.2\%$ of the training
variance; two components reach $96.3\%$, four reach $99.1\%$, and nine
exceed $99.9\%$. From the seventh component onward, each contributes less
than $10^{-3}$ of the total variance. Most variation is therefore
concentrated in a few shared patterns, with progressively smaller
corrections in later components. This concerns variance in log-amplitude,
not a fraction of cosmological information.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{pca_explained_variance.pdf}
  \caption{Training PCA spectrum of the stored rank-30 basis. Left:
  individual explained-variance fractions on a logarithmic axis.
  Right: cumulative fraction, with the first
  ranks exceeding $95\%$, $99\%$ and $99.9\%$ identified. The cumulative
  fraction at rank 30 is approximately $0.999993$. These training fractions
  differ from recovered variance measured on validation surfaces.}
  \label{fig:pca-variance}
\end{figure}

Small-variance components can nevertheless matter for local accuracy.
Figure~\ref{fig:pca-rank} illustrates this on 256 validation surfaces.
Separately, evaluations on all 10,000 validation surfaces give rank 10
a mean relative error of $1.04\%$, yet a
maximum of $29.04\%$. Retaining 30 components reduces the mean to
$0.072\%$ and the maximum to $4.50\%$, with log RMSE
$5.38\times10^{-4}$ dex. Among the tested ranks 25--30, rank 28 is the
first below the $5\%$ maximum-error reference. The later components
therefore make a material difference even though they carry little total
variance. Rank-30 PCA is more accurate than the six-coordinate models; its
role in PCA-AE is to provide an accurate initial projection before further
compression.

\subsection{Residual errors of the compact Conv1D model}
\label{sec:results-residuals}

The original notebook figures in this and the next subsection describe
the earlier three-layer, six-coordinate run \texttt{20260823-103003},
checkpoint epoch 561. They use the same training and validation samples
and seed 42 as the September comparison, but different learned weights.
This earlier run recovers $99.92\%$ of the validation variance, with
mean relative error $1.25\%$ and maximum error $25.57\%$.
The figures illustrate the error structure and learned coordinates of
this earlier fit; its scores are not substituted into
Table~\ref{tab:validation-results}, and the two same-seed runs do not
establish variation across independent seeds.

Figure~\ref{fig:ae-diagnostics} locates the errors of the six-coordinate
Conv1D reconstruction. The mean and maximum maps show enhanced errors near
the redshift boundaries, particularly at $z=0$, across part of the
scale-transition region, and towards the high-$k$ boundary. Their colour
scales differ: colour brightness cannot be compared directly between the
maps. The lower panels quantify how often large errors occur. Five
percent of validation surfaces have relative RMS above $3.21\%$; one
percent have a maximum local error above $17.17\%$. These are different
summaries of the error distribution and need not identify the same surfaces.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{ae_validation_diagnostics.pdf}
  \caption{Complete-validation error diagnostics for the three-layer
  Conv1D model at $L=6$, run \texttt{20260823-103003}, checkpoint epoch
  561, using the original notebook export. Top: mean and maximum absolute
  relative error at each $(k,z)$
  location, with separate logarithmic colour scales. Wavenumber is shown
  on a logarithmic horizontal axis. Bottom: fractions of surfaces with
  errors at or above each horizontal-axis value, for surface relative
  RMS and surface maximum error. Dashed and dotted lines mark their
  respective 95th and 99th percentiles.}
  \label{fig:ae-diagnostics}
\end{figure}

These patterns are consistent with the model's freedom to move the onset
and high-$k$ behaviour of $S(k,z)$ and to change its redshift dependence.
However, the maps alone do not show whether the residuals arise mainly
from limited representation size, the training objective, sampling, or
network architecture. Near the large-scale limit, $S$ approaches unity,
but the target surface still varies through $R_zR_L$. It would therefore
be incorrect to treat every large-scale grid value as an identical input.

The three worst stored cases in Figure~\ref{fig:ae-worst} retain the broad
structure of the target while showing spatially concentrated residuals.
Their maximum errors are much larger than the population mean. A short
representation can consequently be useful for typical surfaces while
remaining inadequate for a demanding local-error requirement. The
$99.9\%$ variance target does not resolve that distinction.

\subsection{Latent geometry and parameter associations}
\label{sec:results-latents}

The latent plots provide exploratory diagnostics of the learned
representation. Figure~\ref{fig:ae-latent-geometry} shows two projections
of the six-dimensional validation sample after centring and scaling each
coordinate using its training distribution. The colours locate
high-error surfaces in these projections, but two views cannot describe
the full distribution. The lower-left panel compares medians and central
68\% intervals between training and validation, rather than showing
complete marginal densities. Similar intervals indicate stability of
these summaries; neither they nor an approximately elliptical projection
establish a Gaussian latent distribution.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{ae_latent_geometry.pdf}
  \caption{Exploratory latent diagnostics for the August six-coordinate
  Conv1D checkpoint in Figure~\ref{fig:ae-diagnostics}.
  Top: validation latent 1 versus latent 2, coloured by surface
  relative RMS, and latent 1 versus latent 3, coloured by surface maximum
  error. Lighter colours indicate larger errors, with
  logarithmic colour scales. Bottom left: coordinate medians and
  16th--84th percentile intervals for training and validation, using the
  same training standardization. Bottom right: Pearson correlations
  between the six validation coordinates.}
  \label{fig:ae-latent-geometry}
\end{figure}

Several latent coordinates are correlated with one another. This indicates
linear association between their variations, but does not measure the
number of independent physical directions. The latent axes are also not
unique: rotating or otherwise reparameterizing a code can preserve its
reconstruction while changing individual correlations.
Figure~\ref{fig:ae-latent-corr} shows associations with the generating
parameters. The most visible include broad redshift evolution and
scale-response controls, consistent with their effects in
Section~\ref{sec:model}. A coordinate can combine several parameter
influences, and a parameter can affect several coordinates.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{ae_latent_parameter_correlations.pdf}
  \caption{Pearson correlations between the six Conv1D latent coordinates
  and the 13 shape parameters on the same 10,000 validation surfaces,
  for the August checkpoint in Figure~\ref{fig:ae-diagnostics}.
  Red and blue indicate positive and negative correlations. Numerical
  annotations are shown for $|r|\geq0.35$ as a readability choice, not a
  significance threshold. The transition wavenumber uses its sampled
  physical value $k_{t,*}$, not its logarithm.}
  \label{fig:ae-latent-corr}
\end{figure}

A weak Pearson correlation does not imply that a parameter is absent:
its influence may be nonlinear or distributed across coordinates.
Counting strong entries cannot establish a physical dimension. Six
coordinates are justified here by reconstruction accuracy.
Parameter-conditioned errors, controlled parameter changes, and nonlinear
prediction from the full code would provide stronger tests of which
variations are retained.

\subsection{Limitations and the next methodological tests}
\label{sec:results-limitations}

The main limitation is the restricted response family. The functions and
sampling ranges are design choices, not constraints from simulations or
galaxy observations. The positive shape surface and $q\geq0$ permit
scale-dependent enhancement above the redshift-only baseline but no
suppression below it. A fixed-sign response also cannot describe an
alignment signal that changes sign across the grid. The tested accuracy
therefore applies within this family and its sampling distribution.

The reported neural configurations each have one seed. Validation data
have guided checkpoint, width, and model choices, so their scores can be
optimistic as estimates of final performance. The next priority is to
repeat the compact Conv1D and PCA-AE comparisons with independent seeds,
including a wider bottleneck as an accuracy reference. Learning curves at
several training-set sizes would then test whether more samples improve
rare cases. Targeted sampling or weighting may help difficult regions,
but its benefit must be measured on a fixed validation distribution so
that changes in the population do not masquerade as improved accuracy.
A residual architecture \citep{He2016} is one possible controlled
alternative; additional complexity should be justified by a reproducible
gain. Final choices should then be frozen before evaluating the reserved
test set.

The present encoder and decoder reconstruct supplied surfaces; they do
not provide a calibrated probability distribution from which to draw
new ones. A normalizing flow could learn the joint latent distribution
through invertible transformations of a simple base density
\citep{Papamakarios2021}. Decoded samples would require checks of both
surface fidelity and the induced parameter prior. Reducing pairwise
correlations would not by itself establish independence or recover
physical parameters. A prior learned from the present sampling box would
represent that design distribution, not observational knowledge of IA.

Broader applications also require a broader physical target. TATT includes
additional contributions to alignment spectra
\citep{2019PhRvD.100j3506B}, while halo models describe dependencies on
central and satellite populations and their properties
\citep{Fortuna2021}. Such predictions need not satisfy the single-response
relation in Equation~\eqref{eq:nla-power-spectra}. Extending this approach
may therefore require compressing several signed spectra jointly, with
cosmology and galaxy-population information supplied explicitly. This is
more than replacing one positive amplitude in the present model.
