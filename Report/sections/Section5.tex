\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

All autoencoder numbers in this section are validation results from the
latest run of each cell.  The test split has not been read.

\subsection{PCA: the linear compression baseline}

PCA finds orthogonal linear patterns that account for the largest variation in
the training surfaces.  Each surface is flattened from
$31\times101$ to 3,131 values, centered using the training mean, and projected
onto the leading patterns.  Two components retain $96.23\%$ of the validation
variance.  Four, six, eight, and ten components retain $99.15\%$, $99.76\%$,
$99.87\%$, and $99.94\%$.  Separately, the rank-25 PCA fit accounts for
$99.9986\%$ of training variance.

Figure~\ref{fig:pca-variance} shows the individual and cumulative training
variance, while Figure~\ref{fig:pca-rank} shows validation reconstruction
error as the rank increases.  Most of the gain appears in the first few
components.

\subsection{Conv1D reconstructions at five latent widths}

The Conv1D numbers below are the latest three-layer runs
(\texttt{20260909-003908}), trained with the template in
Section~\ref{sec:autoencoder}.  At $L=2$ the best checkpoint recovers
$98.65\%$ of the validation variance, with log RMSE $0.0319$, mean relative
error $5.30\%$, and a 95th-percentile per-surface relative RMSE of
$11.99\%$.  That improves on two-component PCA, but it does not meet the
$99.9\%$ recovered-variance target.

A wider bottleneck does.  At $L=6$ the recovered variance is $99.92\%$,
with log RMSE $0.00776$ and mean relative error $1.21\%$.  At $L=10$ the
run uses the full 1,000-epoch budget and recovers $99.997\%$, with log RMSE
$0.00148$ and mean relative error $0.23\%$.  Four- and five-layer networks
trained on the same schedule give comparable validation errors at the same
$L$; the table reports the three-layer family.

At every reported width the nonlinear maps improve on PCA.  The gain is
largest at $L=2$, where Conv1D recovers $2.4$ percentage points more
validation variance than two-component PCA.  From $L=6$ onward the three
methods are closer on the average metrics, and Conv1D and PCA-AE nearly
coincide, but Conv1D remains more accurate in the tails: at $L=6$ its
largest relative error is $26.4\%$, against $48.1\%$ for PCA-AE.  Ten
linear components still leave a mean relative error of $1.04\%$, while
ten Conv1D coordinates reduce that to $0.23\%$.

\subsection{Latent-space interpretation}

We have not yet mapped the latent coordinates against the 13 input
parameters, so no coordinate is given a physical interpretation.  Such an
analysis should examine both parameter correlations and reconstructed surfaces
along paths through the latent space.

\subsection{Limitations}

These results have four main limitations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The analytic response is a deliberately flexible, phenomenological
        model family.  It has not yet been calibrated to a particular survey or
        shown to describe all physical intrinsic-alignment effects.
  \item The numbers above come from validation data.  The untouched test
        split will be evaluated only after the architecture and latent
        dimension have been chosen.
  \item Each cell is a single seed.  Deterministic kernels were not
        enforced, so the quoted run is a named trajectory, not a
        multi-seed distribution.
  \item Reconstruction accuracy varies across surfaces, so the mean metrics do
        not establish adequate precision at every scale, redshift, or
        parameter-space location.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Next steps}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item repeat the most promising Conv1D cells with several random seeds;
  \item map reconstruction error over $(k,z)$ and over the 13-parameter space;
  \item test whether a physically motivated weighted loss improves important
        regions without worsening the rest of the surface; and
  \item after selecting the method, evaluate the untouched test set.
\end{enumerate}
