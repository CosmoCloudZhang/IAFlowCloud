\subsection{PCA: the linear compression baseline}

PCA finds fixed orthogonal patterns that explain the largest amount of variation
in the training surfaces.  Each surface is flattened from
$31\times101$ to 3,131 values, centered using the training mean, and projected
onto the leading patterns.  The first two PCA components retain about 96.23\%
of validation variance.  Ten components retain about 99.94\%, while the full
30-component model captures 99.9993\% of training variance.  On all 10,000
validation surfaces, rank 28 is the smallest evaluated basis below our 5\%
global maximum-relative-error ceiling.  We adopt rank 30 for a small safety
margin; its validation maximum relative error is 4.50\%.

\subsection{Preliminary two-dimensional autoencoder result}

The best checkpoint of the current Conv1D Depth03 run occurs at epoch 260 and
recovers 98.42\% of the total validation variance with a two-number latent
space.  Its validation RMSE is 0.03450 in
$\log_{10}\mathcal A_\Theta$, and its mean relative error after returning to
physical amplitude is 5.82\%.  The 95th percentile of the per-surface relative
RMSE is 12.67\%.

At equal latent dimension, the nonlinear autoencoder reduces log-amplitude MSE
by 58.05\%, log-amplitude RMSE by 35.23\%, mean physical relative error by
32.79\%, and the p95 per-surface relative RMSE by 40.99\% compared with rank-2
PCA.  This supports the idea that the surface family has useful nonlinear
structure.  It does not show that two numbers are sufficient for every
scientific use: the 99.9\% variance target was not reached, and tail errors
remain much larger than the average error.

\subsection{Limitations}

The current conclusions have several boundaries:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The analytic response is a deliberately flexible, phenomenological
        model family.  It has not yet been calibrated to a particular survey or
        shown to describe all physical intrinsic-alignment effects.
  \item The autoencoder numbers above come from validation data.  The untouched
        test split must be evaluated only after all model choices are frozen.
  \item The current selected two-dimensional run did not reach the chosen
        99.9\% recovered-variance target, and one selected seed is not enough
        for a full robustness claim.
  \item An average reconstruction metric does not guarantee that every scale,
        redshift, or extreme parameter combination is accurate enough for a
        later cosmological analysis.
  \item A two-dimensional latent space is easy to visualize but may be too
        narrow for the desired precision.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future improvements}

The next experiments should be ordered by what they teach us:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item finish a controlled latent-dimension study, for example dimensions 2,
        4, 8, and 10, using the same data and metrics;
  \item repeat the most promising model with several random seeds;
  \item map reconstruction error over $(k,z)$ and over the 13-parameter space;
  \item test whether a scientifically motivated weighted loss improves weak
        but important regions without worsening the rest of the surface; and
  \item only then investigate a VAE or a density model for sampling the latent
        space.
\end{enumerate}
