\section{Building the intrinsic-alignment model and dataset}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Separating the overall amplitude from the surface shape}

We write the signed intrinsic-alignment amplitude as

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A_{\rm IA}(k,z)
  = -A_0\,\mathcal A_\Omega(z)\,\mathcal A_\Theta(k,z).
  \label{eq:ia_decomposition}
\end{equation}

Here $A_0$ is an overall multiplier, $\mathcal A_\Omega(z)$ contains the
standard cosmological redshift dependence, and $\mathcal A_\Theta(k,z)>0$ is
the flexible shape surface studied in this project.  We compress
$\log_{10}\mathcal A_\Theta$, not the signed full amplitude.  Keeping $A_0$
outside the 13-dimensional shape model avoids asking the network to learn a
simple overall rescaling.

\subsection{Redshift and luminosity factors}

Let

\begin{equation}
  r(z)=\frac{1+z}{1+z_*},
  \qquad
  r_q=\frac{1+z_q}{1+z_*},
\end{equation}

where $z_*$ is a fixed pivot redshift.  The broad redshift response and the
smooth luminosity-like transition are

\begin{align}
  R_z(z) &= r(z)^\eta,\\
  R_L(z) &=
  \left[
    \frac{1+[r(z)/r_q]^s}{1+(1/r_q)^s}
  \right]^{\xi/s}.
  \label{eq:redshift_luminosity}
\end{align}

The parameter $\eta$ tilts the overall redshift trend.  The parameter $\xi$
sets the strength and direction of the second trend, $z_q$ chooses where it
turns, and $s$ controls how sharply the turn happens.

\subsection{Scale dependence and its redshift evolution}

The characteristic scale and shape parameters evolve as

\begin{align}
  k_t(z) &= k_{t,*}\,r(z)^{\gamma_t}, &
  n(z) &= n_*\,r(z)^{\gamma_n},\\
  \alpha(z) &= \alpha\,r(z)^{\gamma_\alpha}, &
  m(z) &= m_*\,r(z)^{\gamma_m}.
\end{align}

With $x=k/k_t(z)$, the scale response is

\begin{equation}
  S(k,z)=1+q\,
  \frac{x^{n(z)}}{1+x^{n(z)}}
  \left(\frac{1+x^{m(z)}}{2}\right)^{\alpha(z)/m(z)}.
  \label{eq:scale_response}
\end{equation}

The full positive surface is

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A_\Theta(k,z)=R_z(z)R_L(z)S(k,z).
  \label{eq:shape_surface}
\end{equation}

This construction is useful because its parts have understandable roles.  For
example, $k_{t,*}$ moves the transition left or right in scale, $q$ changes the
size of the response, $n_*$ controls the transition sharpness, and $\alpha$
changes the high-$k$ slope.  The four $\gamma$ parameters allow these features
to evolve with redshift.

\subsection{Sampling, physical checks, and the data grid}

We draw 100,000 parameter combinations from the ranges in
Table~\ref{tab:priors}.  Every combination produces a surface on 31 redshift
points from $z=0$ to $z=3$ and 101 logarithmically spaced wavenumbers from
$k=0.01$ to $10\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$.  Before generating the full dataset, the
code checks both random points and all $2^{13}=8{,}192$ corners of the parameter
box.  It rejects non-finite, non-positive, or excessively extreme responses.

\subsection{Training, validation, and test subsets}

The stored split contains 80,000 training surfaces, 10,000 validation surfaces,
and 10,000 test surfaces.  The training subset fits model parameters.  The
validation subset chooses and diagnoses the method.  The test subset must stay
unseen until all choices are frozen.  Normalization statistics and the PCA
basis are therefore calculated from training data only.
