\section{Compressing the surfaces with an autoencoder}
\label{sec:autoencoder}

The surfaces of Section~\ref{sec:model} are compressed with a
one-dimensional convolutional autoencoder.  The bottleneck width is
$L\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$.  Section~\ref{sec:results} compares the reconstructions
with PCA at the same $L$.  A second family, PCA-AE, is trained on the same
data; Appendix~\ref{app:pca-ae} records its structure.

\subsection{Input transformation and normalization}

The encoder is given
\begin{equation}
  Y(k,z)=\log_{10}\mathcal A_\Theta(k,z),
\end{equation}
because the positive amplitudes span a wide range, and then the centered
and scaled log-surface
\begin{equation}
  \bm{x}=\frac{Y-\overline{Y}}{\sigma}.
\end{equation}
Here $\overline{Y}$ is the mean training surface and $\sigma$ is the global
root-mean-square deviation from that surface.  Both statistics are computed
from the training split and applied unchanged to the validation and test
data.  A single global scale preserves relative feature sizes across the
grid and avoids leakage from the held-out subsets.

\subsection{Model design strategy}

The encoder maps a normalized surface to a latent vector, and the decoder
reconstructs the input,
\begin{equation}
  \bm{z}=f_{\phi}(\bm{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{L},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\bm{x}}=g_{\theta}(\bm{z}).
\end{equation}
No skip connection bypasses the bottleneck.  Training the same family at
five widths shows how reconstruction accuracy depends on the length of that
description.

The input is arranged as 31 redshift channels, each a 101-point sequence in
wavenumber.  A one-dimensional convolution slides along $k$ with a shared
kernel and mixes neighboring scales; the filters also mix the redshift
channels.  That uses more of the surface structure than treating the 3,131
values as an unrelated list.

Capacity is set by the number of convolutional layers.  We train three-,
four-, and five-layer networks.  Deeper stacks downsample $k$ further and
widen the dense path that follows.  This section uses the three-layer
network as the example.

PCA-AE first projects each surface onto a frozen rank-30 PCA basis and then
compresses those coefficients with a small multilayer perceptron.  It is a
cheaper nonlinear alternative: later results can test whether Conv1D does
more than a short nonlinear code of a good linear basis.  The architecture,
training template, and metrics below refer to Conv1D.

\subsection{A three-layer Conv1D example}

Figure~\ref{fig:ae-architecture} shows the three-layer encoder and its
mirrored decoder.  Three stride-2 convolutions, with kernels of length 5, 5,
and 3, map the $31\times101$ input to a $256\times13$ feature map.  That
map is flattened and reduced through dense widths 256, 64, and 16 to the
square latent block of width $L$.  The decoder reverses this path with a
dense stack and transposed convolutions, and it must rebuild the original
grid.  The network uses SiLU activations, with no dropout, no internal
normalization, and no skip connections.

\subsection{Training strategy}

The network minimizes the mean squared reconstruction error in normalized
log-amplitude space,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\rm MSE}
  =\frac{1}{N N_z N_k}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N_z}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_k}
  \left(\widehat{x}_{ij\ell}-x_{ij\ell}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the batch size, $N_z=31$, and $N_k=101$.
Table~\ref{tab:training-config-details} is the shared template for every
Conv1D depth and every $L$.

AdamW uses a learning rate of $2\times10^{-4}$: a much larger step
oscillates on this normalized MSE, while a much smaller step stalls before
the plateau rule can help.  The weight decay $10^{-5}$ is a light penalty,
so the filters cannot grow without bound.  A batch of 512 gives about 156
updates per epoch on 80,000 surfaces.  Smaller batches make each gradient
noisier and can explore more freely, but they are slower and make the
validation loss less stable from epoch to epoch.  The learning rate is
halved after 12 epochs without improvement, down to $10^{-7}$.  That wait
is long enough to ignore ordinary batch noise and short enough to allow
several reductions before the 50-epoch early-stopping window closes.
Training may run for 1,000 epochs.  Seed 42 labels the initialization and
the batch order; deterministic kernels are not enforced, so the seed names
a run rather than guaranteeing bit-for-bit identity across devices.

\subsection{Validation metrics}

A batch-mean loss can hide a small region of the grid, or a difficult
surface, with a much larger error.  After each run we therefore compute the
summaries in Table~\ref{tab:validation-metrics} on the 10,000 validation
surfaces.  Physical relative error is
$\lvert 10^{(\widehat Y-Y)}-1\rvert$.  The table distinguishes typical
error from the tail; the entries are diagnostics, not thresholds.
