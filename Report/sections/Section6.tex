\section{Summary and conclusion}
\label{sec:summary}

This work asked whether a $31\times101$ intrinsic-alignment shape surface
can be replaced by a short learned description, and whether that
description improves on PCA at the same length.  A phenomenological NLA
family supplies 100,000 surfaces from 13 sampled shape parameters.  PCA
is the linear baseline.  A one-dimensional convolutional autoencoder,
and a cheaper PCA autoencoder, are trained at
$L\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$.

The conclusion is that a nonlinear compressor is useful for this family,
and that six Conv1D coordinates are enough for the average target.  At
equal $L$ the three-layer Conv1D network improves on PCA:
it recovers $98.65\%$ of the validation variance at $L=2$ against
$96.23\%$ for two-component PCA, and $99.92\%$ at $L=6$, which already
approaches ten-component PCA ($99.94\%$). The original notebook
diagnostics of the earlier August fit show associations between its
latents and the sampled parameters. Their number does not establish
the physical dimension; reconstruction accuracy supports the compact
choice. At $L=6$, PCA-AE has comparable averages and percentile errors,
while Conv1D has the smaller observed global maximum error.

What the same numbers do not show is a representation that is already
safe for cosmology.  The ranking is a single-seed validation ranking;
the test split has not been read.  Mean relative error at $L=6$ is
$1.21\%$, but the hardest surfaces still reach a $26\%$ local error.
The original notebook maps of the August fit locate enhanced residuals
at low redshift, around the scale transition, and towards high $k$.
Its latent projections do not establish a Gaussian distribution,
and weak pairwise correlations do not show that a physical parameter
has been lost. The surfaces
themselves come from a hand-specified model, not from a simulation or
a survey.

Section~\ref{sec:results} already lists the next tests of the
compressor, the latent prior, and a more physical alignment family.
If a short code of this kind survives those tests, it can enter a
weak-lensing analysis as a compact alignment nuisance: fewer free
functions to sample, and a clearer separation between alignment
uncertainty and the cosmological signal in Stage-IV surveys.
