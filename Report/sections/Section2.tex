\subsection{Cosmology and the contents of the Universe}

Cosmology is the study of the Universe as a whole: how it began, how it changes
with time, and how galaxies and larger structures formed.  Ordinary matter
makes stars, planets, and people, but it is only one part of the cosmic picture.
Two other ideas are especially important:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dark matter} does not shine, but its gravity helps to hold
        galaxies together and build the large-scale structure of the Universe.
  \item \textbf{Dark energy} is the name given to the unknown cause associated
        with the accelerated expansion of the Universe.
\end{itemize}

We study these components because their properties affect both the history of
cosmic expansion and the pattern of matter that forms over time.

\subsection{Gravity changes the path of light}

According to general relativity, matter changes the geometry through which
light travels.  A large concentration of matter can therefore bend the light
from a more distant galaxy.  Strong lensing can create obvious arcs or multiple
images.  Weak lensing is much smaller: a single galaxy does not show a clear
effect, so astronomers look for a shared statistical pattern across many
galaxies.

\subsection{From galaxy shapes to a weak-lensing signal}

A galaxy image can be described approximately by its ellipticity, which records
how stretched it looks and in which direction.  The original shape of any one
galaxy is unknown.  However, if many galaxies have nearly random original
orientations, averaging their measured shapes can reveal the small lensing
distortion.  Correlations between pairs of galaxy shapes are therefore a key
observable.

\subsection{Intrinsic alignment: a false lensing pattern}

The assumption of random original orientations is not exact.  Galaxies that
form in the same large-scale gravitational environment can have related shapes
or directions.  This intrinsic alignment creates correlations even before
lensing is applied, and it can mix with the true lensing signal.

It is useful to separate three contributions conceptually:

\begin{description}
  \item[GG:] two galaxy images are correlated by gravitational lensing;
  \item[II:] two intrinsic galaxy shapes are correlated with one another;
  \item[GI:] the intrinsic shape of one galaxy is correlated with the lensing
             of another galaxy.
\end{description}

The goal of this project is not to remove intrinsic alignment from real survey
data.  Instead, we develop and compress a flexible family of possible
intrinsic-alignment responses that could later be used in a larger analysis.

\subsection{Why compress a surface into a few numbers?}

Our model produces an amplitude $\mathcal A_\Theta(k,z)$ at many wavenumbers
$k$ and redshifts $z$.  The full grid is useful for plotting, but repeatedly
carrying 3,131 values through later calculations is expensive.  Nearby grid
points are also strongly related, so the surface may contain much less than
3,131 independent pieces of information.

Dimensionality reduction searches for a shorter description.  PCA represents
each surface as a weighted sum of fixed linear patterns.  An autoencoder learns
a nonlinear map into a low-dimensional \emph{latent space}.  Comparing them at
the same latent dimension lets us ask whether nonlinear structure really helps.
